## 0
Q: Which are the six functions of NIST CSF 2.0?
* Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover
- Plan, Do, Check, Act, Report, Audit
- Prevent, Detect, Correct, Deter, Direct, Compensate
- Assess, Authorize, Categorize, Select, Implement, Monitor
? Govern was added in version 2.0.

Q: Which framework is an international standard that organizations can be certified against?
* ISO/IEC 27001
- NIST CSF
- CIS Controls
- MITRE ATT&CK
? CSF and CIS are not certifiable.

Q: In NIST SP 800-53, which family ID is Access Control?
* AC
- AU
- IA
- SC
? AU is audit, IA is identification and authentication, SC is system and communications protection.

Q: What is a crosswalk?
* A mapping between the controls of different frameworks
- A physical security barrier
- A type of audit
- A risk score
? One MFA control can satisfy NIST, ISO and CIS items.

Q: A badge reader on a data center door is best described as:
* A physical, preventive control
- A technical, detective control
- A managerial, corrective control
- An operational, deterrent control
? Security+ expects control categories and types.

## 1
Q: What is the formula for annualized loss expectancy?
* ALE = SLE x ARO
- ALE = AV x EF
- ALE = SLE + ARO
- ALE = ARO / SLE
? SLE is AV x EF, and ARO is events per year.

Q: An asset is worth $400,000, one event destroys 10% of it, and the event happens once every 2 years. What is the ALE?
* $20,000
- $40,000
- $80,000
- $200,000
? SLE = 40,000; ARO = 0.5; ALE = 20,000.

Q: A company buys cyber insurance to cover a risk. Which treatment is this?
* Transfer
- Mitigate
- Accept
- Avoid
? Insurance shifts the financial cost, not the responsibility.

Q: A control costs $4,000 a year and lowers ALE from $12,500 to $2,500. What is the yearly net benefit?
* $6,000
- $10,000
- $8,500
- $2,500
? 12,500 - 2,500 - 4,000 = 6,000.

Q: What belongs in a good risk register entry?
* A specific risk, an owner, likelihood, impact, treatment and review date
- Only the names of attackers
- A list of tools
- The firewall rules
? Every risk needs one owner who can decide about it.

## 2
Q: Which document layer is mandatory, specific and measurable, such as "use AES-256 full-disk encryption"?
* Standard
- Guideline
- Policy
- Procedure
? A policy says what and why, a standard says how much, a procedure says how exactly.

Q: Which wording makes a policy statement enforceable?
* Passwords must be at least 14 characters.
- Passwords should be reasonably strong.
- Use appropriate password measures.
- Passwords ought to be good.
? An auditor must be able to mark it pass or fail.

Q: What is a guideline?
* A recommended practice that is not mandatory
- A law
- A signed contract
- A technical control
? Guidelines advise; policies and standards require.

Q: Why must policy exceptions be recorded with an expiry date?
* So accepted risk stays visible, owned and reviewed
- Because exceptions are illegal
- To avoid training employees
- To save storage
? Exceptions belong in the risk register.

Q: What gives a policy its authority in an organization?
* Approval and sign-off from executive leadership
- A long length
- Technical jargon
- Being printed in color
? Without sign-off it is only a suggestion.

## 3
Q: A SOC 2 Type II report tests controls:
* Over a period of time, such as 6 to 12 months
- At a single point in time only
- Only for payment cards
- By self-assessment
? Type I covers design at one date.

Q: Which standard applies to organizations that store, process or transmit payment card data?
* PCI DSS
- FERPA
- SOX
- COPPA
? PCI DSS is contractual, enforced through the card brands.

Q: Under HIPAA, a vendor that handles PHI for a provider signs a:
* BAA
- NDA only
- SLA
- DPIA
? Business associate agreement.

Q: Which evidence is strongest in an audit?
* Dated, attributable output from the system of record
- A verbal statement that the control exists
- An undated screenshot of a different system
- A policy that was never applied
? Evidence must be dated, attributable and complete.

Q: What is the difference between design effectiveness and operating effectiveness?
* Whether the control is appropriate versus whether it worked consistently over the period
- Whether it is cheap versus expensive
- Whether it is technical versus physical
- Whether it is new versus old
? Auditors test both.

## 4
Q: Under GDPR, how soon must a notifiable personal data breach be reported to the supervisory authority?
* Within 72 hours of becoming aware
- Within 30 days
- Within 60 days
- Within one year
? Individuals are told without undue delay if the risk to them is high.

Q: Under GDPR, which party decides why and how personal data is processed?
* The controller
- The processor
- The data subject
- The DPO
? A processor acts on the controller's behalf.

Q: Which US law covers student education records?
* FERPA
- HIPAA
- GLBA
- COPPA
? HIPAA covers health information.

Q: Why is a lost, properly encrypted laptop usually not a reportable breach?
* Encryption provides a safe harbor because the data is unreadable
- Laptops are exempt from all laws
- Encrypted data is public
- The law only covers servers
? Keep the key escrow and encryption evidence.

Q: What is data minimization?
* Collecting and keeping only the personal data you need
- Compressing files to save space
- Encrypting every field
- Backing up less often
? Data you do not hold cannot be breached.

## 5
Q: What is coordinated (responsible) disclosure?
* Reporting a flaw privately to the owner and publishing after a fix or an agreed deadline
- Posting it publicly at once
- Selling it to the highest bidder
- Keeping it secret forever
? A deadline of about 90 days is a common norm.

Q: Where is the standard place for a site to list its security contact?
* /.well-known/security.txt
- /robots.txt
- /admin
- /sitemap.xml
? It tells researchers where to report.

Q: You notice customer records exposed on a company's public page. What is the best action?
* Stop at minimal evidence and report it without downloading the data
- Download everything as proof
- Post screenshots online
- Ask for payment to stay quiet
? Least data and no extortion.

Q: Which US law makes unauthorized access to a computer a crime?
* The Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA)
- FERPA
- SOX
- COPPA
? Authorization is the line, so get permission in writing.

Q: A client asks you to soften a finding so the CEO is not embarrassed. What is the ethical response?
* Report the finding accurately and discuss the wording, not the truth
- Remove the finding
- Change the severity quietly
- Delete your notes
? Honesty is a core professional principle.
