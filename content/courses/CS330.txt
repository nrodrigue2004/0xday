## 0
Q: Which sentence best describes a machine learning model?
* A formula with adjustable numbers, tuned on examples until it sorts them well
- A program with every rule written by hand
- A database of all past attacks
- A system that understands what it reads
? The model does not understand anything; it fits numbers to the examples you gave it.

Q: In a spam filter, what is a label?
* The right answer for an example, such as spam or not spam
- The number of links in the email
- The model's confidence score
- The name of the algorithm
? Features are the inputs; the label is the answer you want the model to learn.

Q: Which is a feature of an email?
* The number of links it contains
- Whether a human marked it as spam
- The accuracy of the filter
- The training date
? A feature is a measurable fact about the example, available at prediction time.

Q: What is the difference between training and prediction?
* Training adjusts the model's numbers using labeled examples; prediction uses the finished model on new data
- Training is fast and prediction is slow
- Prediction needs labels and training does not
- They are the same step
? Learning happens in training only. Prediction is a cheap calculation with the numbers frozen.

Q: A model scores 100 percent on its training data and 70 percent on held-out test data. What happened?
* It overfit: it memorized the training examples instead of the pattern
- It underfit: it is too simple
- The test set is too easy
- Nothing, a drop is normal and always acceptable
? A large gap between train and test is the overfitting signal. Choose models by the test score.

Q: 1,000 files, 50 are malware. A model flags 60, and 40 of those are malware. What is its precision?
* About 67 percent (40 of 60)
- 80 percent
- 97 percent
- 40 percent
? Precision = TP / (TP + FP) = 40 / 60. Recall would be 40 / 50 = 80 percent.

Q: In the same example, what is the recall?
* 80 percent (40 of the 50 real malware files)
- 67 percent
- 97 percent
- 20 percent
? Recall = TP / (TP + FN) = 40 / 50.

Q: Why is accuracy a poor headline number for malware detection?
* Malware is rare, so a model that flags nothing still scores very high
- Accuracy cannot be calculated for two classes
- Accuracy ignores true positives
- It only works with regression
? Always compare against the do-nothing baseline and look at precision and recall.

Q: What does anomaly detection do?
* Learns what normal looks like and flags what does not fit
- Sorts files into labeled families using examples
- Predicts a number from other numbers
- Writes detection rules from a vendor feed
? It works without labels but flags "unusual", not "malicious", so expect false alarms.

Q: Why do attackers make ML security tools harder to build than a cat-photo classifier?
* Attackers adapt on purpose to evade the model, and real attacks are rare
- Security data has fewer features
- Models cannot read file data
- Cat photos need more training data
? Evasion, rare positives, biased labels and drift are the recurring problems.

## 1
Q: What does the dot product of a weight vector and a feature vector give you in a model?
* A weighted sum of the evidence: the score
- The distance between two points
- The number of features
- The learning rate
? w . x multiplies matching positions and adds them up.

Q: A has shape (4, 3) and B has shape (3, 2). What is the shape of A @ B?
* (4, 2)
- (3, 3)
- (4, 3)
- (3, 2)
? The inner sizes must match and disappear.

Q: What does the gradient tell you in training?
* Which direction increases the loss fastest, so you step the opposite way
- The best learning rate
- The number of epochs needed
- Which features are useless
? new_w = w - learning_rate * gradient.

Q: What happens if the learning rate is far too large?
* Steps overshoot and the loss can bounce or diverge
- Training becomes more accurate
- Nothing, it only changes speed
- The model gets regularized
? Too small and training crawls instead.

Q: Why scale features before gradient descent or nearest neighbors?
* Features on very different scales dominate the result
- It makes the labels binary
- It removes the need for a test set
- It reduces the number of rows
? A count in the thousands next to a 0/1 flag distorts distance and slows descent.

Q: A login risk scorer has weights [2, 3, -4] and values [5, 1, 1]. What is the score w . x?
* 9
- 2
- 15
- 3
? 2*5 + 3*1 + (-4)*1 = 10 + 3 - 4 = 9.

## 2
Q: What loss does ordinary linear regression minimize?
* Mean squared error
- Accuracy
- Cross-entropy
- Precision
? The average of the squared gaps between prediction and truth.

Q: Why keep a test set that you do not touch while tuning?
* To get an honest estimate of performance on unseen data
- To train faster
- To increase the training set
- To avoid scaling features
? Scores on the training data only show how well the model memorized it.

Q: A model has very low training error and high test error. What is this?
* Overfitting
- Underfitting
- Data drift
- A bad learning rate only
? The model learned the noise. More data, simpler models or regularization help.

Q: You are predicting next week's log volume from past weeks. How should you split train and test?
* By time: train on the past, test on the future
- Randomly shuffle all rows
- Put the newest data in training
- Use only the test set
? Shuffling leaks the future into training.

Q: What does L2 (ridge) regularization do?
* Penalizes large weights so the model stays simpler
- Adds more training data
- Removes the test set
- Changes the labels
? Lambda is a hyperparameter you tune on validation data.

Q: Which model do you ship after these results: degree 1 test error 9.8, degree 3 test error 2.6, degree 9 test error 14.7 (training error 0.1)?
* Degree 3, the lowest test error
- Degree 9, the lowest training error
- Degree 1, the simplest
- Any, they are equivalent
? Training error always falls as the model grows; only test error is honest.

## 3
Q: What does the sigmoid function do in logistic regression?
* Squeezes the weighted sum into a probability between 0 and 1
- Scales the features
- Chooses the learning rate
- Counts the classes
? Then a threshold turns the probability into a decision.

Q: What kind of boundary can plain logistic regression draw?
* A straight line (a flat plane in more dimensions)
- Any curve
- Only circles
- No boundary
? That is why it cannot separate two concentric rings without extra features.

Q: Why is a random forest usually better than a single deep tree?
* Averaging many varied trees reduces variance and overfitting
- It needs no data
- It trains with one example
- Its trees are all identical
? Each tree sees random rows and features.

Q: Which classic classifier counts how often words appear in spam versus normal mail?
* Naive Bayes
- k-means
- Linear regression
- PCA
? It multiplies the evidence from each word, assuming independence.

Q: For a feature that helps spot domains made by a generator, which is a good choice?
* Character entropy of the domain name
- The server's color
- The order of the letters in the alphabet
- The number of nameservers in a country
? Random-looking names have higher entropy than human-chosen ones.

Q: With k = 3 nearest neighbors, the closest labels are benign, malicious, malicious. What is the prediction?
* Malicious (2 to 1 vote)
- Benign, because the nearest one wins
- It cannot be decided
- Both
? With k = 1 it would say benign; that fragility is why k is tuned on validation data.

## 4
Q: Attacks are 0.1 percent of events. A model that always says benign scores 99.9 percent accuracy. What does it show?
* Accuracy is misleading on rare classes
- The model is excellent
- The test set is too small
- The threshold is too high
? It catches zero attacks. Use precision, recall and the confusion matrix.

Q: Which formula is recall?
* TP / (TP + FN)
- TP / (TP + FP)
- (TP + TN) / all
- FP / (FP + TN)
? Of all real attacks, the share the model caught.

Q: Which formula is precision?
* TP / (TP + FP)
- TP / (TP + FN)
- TN / (TN + FP)
- FN / (TP + FN)
? When it raises an alarm, how often is it right.

Q: You lower the classification threshold. What usually happens?
* Recall rises and precision falls
- Both rise
- Recall falls and precision rises
- Nothing changes
? You catch more attacks and raise more false alarms.

Q: Which is a data leakage example?
* Using a field recorded after the incident was resolved to predict the incident's outcome
- Splitting by time
- Holding out a test set
- Scaling after splitting
? Ask of every feature: will I know this at prediction time?

## 5
Q: In the ML playground, what happens when you train logistic regression on the rings dataset?
* It stays poor, because a straight line cannot separate the rings
- It reaches 100 percent
- It crashes
- It becomes a neural net
? This is underfitting: the model is too simple for the data.

Q: Why does the playground's neural net separate the rings?
* The hidden layer's nonlinearity lets the boundary curve
- It has more training data
- It ignores the labels
- It uses a different threshold
? Stacked linear layers alone would still be linear.

Q: What is poisoning an ML model?
* Slipping mislabeled or crafted samples into the training data
- Lowering the learning rate
- Raising the threshold
- Adding a hidden layer
? It is one of the attacks covered in the attacking-AI chapter.

Q: Why does a deployed detection model need monitoring after launch?
* Inputs drift as software and attackers change, so performance silently decays
- Models improve on their own over time
- Labels never change
- Thresholds update themselves
? Retrain on a schedule and watch alert-to-confirmed ratios.

Q: Moons dataset, high noise, neural net trained for 1000+ epochs: train accuracy far above test accuracy. What does this show?
* Overfitting
- Underfitting
- A leakage-free model
- A perfect model
? The model memorized noise in the training points.
