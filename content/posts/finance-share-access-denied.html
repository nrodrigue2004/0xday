A classic help-desk ticket, reproduced in my Enterprise IT Support Lab: a user can sign in to the domain and reach the Finance share, but gets "access denied" when opening it.

Working the ticket

  1. Reproduced the problem from the domain-joined workstation
  2. Checked the user's Active Directory group membership
  3. Found they were missing from Finance-Share-Read
  4. Added them to the group
  5. Had them sign out and back in so Windows issued a fresh security token
  6. Confirmed access to the share
Add-ADGroupMember -Identity "Finance-Share-Read" -Members "atest"
Get-ADPrincipalGroupMembership atest | Select-Object Name

Why sign-out matters

Group membership is baked into the access token at logon. Adding someone to a group does nothing for their current session, which is one of the most common reasons a "fixed" permission still looks broken.

The share used both share-level and NTFS permissions, which is a good reminder that Windows access control is layered: the most restrictive one wins.
