tagline: Compliance is proving to customers, regulators and auditors that you follow the rules. Learn the difference between compliance and security, SOC 2, PCI DSS and HIPAA, how an audit runs and how to collect evidence.
sheet: grc
:: simple
**Compliance** means meeting the requirements of a law, regulation, contract or standard. An **audit** is an independent check that you did. Security is about actually being safe. Compliance is about being able to prove you follow a defined set of rules. They overlap but they are not the same: you can pass an audit and still get breached, and you can be secure and fail an audit for missing paperwork.

### Where requirements come from
- **Laws and regulations**: HIPAA (health), GLBA (financial), SOX (public company financial reporting), GDPR (EU personal data), FERPA (student records)
- **Industry standards with contractual force**: PCI DSS (payment cards)
- **Voluntary frameworks and attestations**: ISO 27001 certification, SOC 2 report
- **Customer contracts**: security questionnaires and addenda

### Types of audits
| Type | Who does it | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal** | your own audit team | find problems early |
| **External** | an independent firm | report to customers or regulators |
| **Regulatory** | the regulator | check legal compliance |
| **Third-party / vendor** | you audit your supplier (or they audit you) | supply chain assurance |

### Three lines of defense
1. The business and IT own and operate controls
2. Risk and compliance teams oversee them
3. Internal audit gives independent assurance to the board

### SOC 2
A report by an independent CPA firm on a service provider's controls, based on the AICPA **Trust Services Criteria**: **Security** (required), **Availability**, **Processing Integrity**, **Confidentiality**, **Privacy**.
- **Type I**: controls are designed properly at a point in time
- **Type II**: controls operated effectively over a period (usually 6 to 12 months). This is what customers usually want
Reports are shared under NDA; they are not a pass/fail certificate. The auditor gives an opinion and lists exceptions.

### PCI DSS
The Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard, for anyone who stores, processes or transmits cardholder data. Version 4.x has **12 principal requirements**, including secure network, no vendor default passwords, protecting stored account data, encrypting data in transit, anti-malware, secure systems and software, restricting access by need to know, authentication, physical access, logging and monitoring, regular testing and an information security policy. Shrink the scope (**tokenization**, hosted payment pages) to reduce the work. Merchants are grouped by volume into levels and may do a self-assessment questionnaire (SAQ) or an on-site assessment by a QSA.

### HIPAA
US law protecting health information (**PHI**). The **Privacy Rule** limits use and disclosure, the **Security Rule** requires administrative, physical and technical safeguards for electronic PHI (risk analysis is mandatory), and the **Breach Notification Rule** requires notice to affected people and regulators. Business associates (vendors who handle PHI) are covered too and sign a **BAA**.

### How an audit runs
1. **Scoping and planning**: what systems, what period, which controls
2. **Request list**: the auditor asks for policies, screenshots, configs, tickets, logs, lists of users
3. **Fieldwork**: sampling and testing (for example, 25 of last quarter's 400 new hires: was access approved before it was granted?)
4. **Findings**: exceptions and observations, with time for management to respond
5. **Report**: opinion or result
6. **Remediation and follow-up**

### Evidence rules of thumb
Evidence must be **dated, attributable, complete and from the system of record**. A screenshot of a settings page with the date visible is good; a statement "we do this" is not. Good evidence is collected continuously, not in panic before the audit.
:: complex
### Control testing
Auditors test **design** (is the control appropriate?) and **operating effectiveness** (did it work every time?). Methods: inquiry, observation, inspection of documents, and reperformance. Samples are chosen by the auditor, not by you. One failed sample is an exception, a pattern is a finding.

### Compliance automation
Tools (Vanta, Drata and similar) and cloud-native services (AWS Config, Azure Policy) pull evidence straight from systems and flag drift. Benefits: continuous assurance and less scrambling. Caveat: tools check configuration, not whether the control is right, so a human still decides scope and risk.

### Common failure modes
Joiner-mover-leaver gaps (access not removed on termination), missing quarterly access reviews, unpatched critical vulnerabilities past the policy deadline, backups never test-restored, policies not reviewed annually, no evidence of training completion, and vendors with no assessment on file.

### Compliance versus risk
Use compliance as the floor and risk as the ceiling. Unique risks (your biggest customer, your riskiest app) may need controls no standard requires. Report both.

### Careers
GRC analyst, compliance analyst, IT auditor (CISA certification), privacy analyst, third-party risk analyst. Security+ gives the vocabulary; CISA, CISSP, CRISC and ISO 27001 Lead Implementer/Auditor are the common next steps. See [~/careers](../careers.html).

### Ethics in auditing
Auditors must be independent and objective, keep what they see confidential, and not alter evidence. If you are audited, never fabricate or backdate evidence. That turns a finding into fraud.

### audit terms
Evidence | dated, attributable proof a control works
Population / sample | all items / the subset tested
Exception | a sampled item that failed
Finding | a control gap reported
Design vs operating effectiveness | is it right / did it work every time
Attestation | formal statement of assurance

:: solution
Two of the six leavers fail. `r.owens` left on Oct 3 and was disabled Oct 9 (6 days), and `s.patel` left Oct 20 and is still enabled. The others were disabled the same day or the next day. In the audit report this is an **exception** at 2 of 6, which is a pattern, so the finding would recommend automating deprovisioning from the HR system and adding a monthly reconciliation.
