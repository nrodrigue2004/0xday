A **threat vector** is the path an attacker uses to get in. The **attack surface** is every place a vector could exist. This objective splits into technical vectors (software, networks, devices) and human vectors (social engineering).

### Common threat vectors

| Vector | What it means | Key defenses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Message-based** | Email, SMS, instant messages, collaboration chat carry links, attachments and scams. Email is the biggest one | Spam and attachment filtering, link rewriting, DMARC/DKIM/SPF, user awareness |
| **Image-based** | Malicious code or links hidden in image files (SVG with scripts, steganography) | Content inspection, don't auto-render untrusted images |
| **File-based** | Documents, PDFs, archives, macros, executables | Sandboxing, disable macros, attachment scanning, application control |
| **Voice call** | Vishing, spoofed caller ID, deepfake voice | Call-back verification, policy, awareness |
| **Removable device** | USB drives dropped in a parking lot, malicious cables, autorun malware | Disable USB ports or autorun, USB data blockers, endpoint control |
| **Vulnerable software** | Unpatched code. **Client-based** (agent installed) or **agentless** (scanned remotely) | Patching, vulnerability scanning |
| **Unsupported systems and applications** | No vendor patches (end of life). Becomes a permanent hole | Replace, isolate, compensating controls |
| **Unsecure network** | Wireless (open Wi-Fi, weak WPA, rogue AP), wired (unprotected ports), Bluetooth (bluejacking, bluesnarfing) | Encryption, 802.1X, NAC, disable unused ports |
| **Open service ports** | Every listening port is an entry point | Close unused ports, host firewall, scan regularly |
| **Default credentials** | admin/admin on routers, cameras, IoT | Change at install, password policy |
| **Supply chain** | Attack through a trusted vendor, MSP or supplier | Vendor assessment, contracts, monitoring |

**Supply chain pieces to recognize:** a **managed service provider (MSP)** has remote access to many customers, so compromising one MSP can reach all of them. A **vendor** supplies hardware or software. A **supplier** provides parts and components. Tainted hardware, counterfeit parts and poisoned updates are all supply chain attacks.

### Human vectors and social engineering

Social engineering manipulates people rather than systems. Attackers rely on psychological levers: **authority, urgency, scarcity, social proof, familiarity/liking, trust, fear and consensus**.

#### Phishing family

- **Phishing**: fraudulent email pretending to be a trusted sender, tricking you into clicking, opening or entering credentials. Clues: wrong or look-alike domain, mismatched link text, generic greeting, urgency, bad grammar (though modern ones are polished), unexpected attachment.
- **Spear phishing**: aimed at a specific person or group with personal details.
- **Whaling**: spear phishing aimed at senior executives.
- **Vishing**: voice phishing by phone, often with spoofed caller ID.
- **Smishing**: phishing by SMS text message.
- **Pharming**: redirects victims to a fake site by corrupting DNS or the hosts file.
- **Typosquatting** (URL hijacking): registers a mistyped domain (`exampel.com`) to catch typos. Related: **brand impersonation** (a site or email using a company's branding).
- **Business email compromise (BEC)**: a spoofed or hijacked executive or vendor email asks staff to wire money or change payment details. Defended with out-of-band verification.

#### Impersonation and pretexting

- **Impersonation**: pretending to be someone else (IT support, auditor, delivery person, executive).
- **Pretexting**: an invented scenario that justifies the request ("I'm from payroll and need to confirm your bank details").
- **Identity fraud**: using someone's stolen identity for gain.
- **Eliciting information**: casual conversation that draws out details without the target noticing.

Defense: policies for verifying identity, call-back procedures, escalation, and a culture where people can say "let me verify that".

#### Watering hole attack

The attacker compromises a website that the target group already visits (an industry forum, a supplier portal) and waits. Visitors get infected with no click on a bad link. Defense: patching, web filtering, browser isolation, endpoint protection, monitoring for odd behavior after visiting trusted sites. This is also why you can't rely on "only visit trusted sites".

#### Other social engineering

- **Misinformation / disinformation**: false information, spread by mistake or on purpose, to influence behavior or reputation.
- **Dumpster diving**: reading discarded paper for data. Shred documents.
- **Shoulder surfing**: watching a screen or keypad. Privacy screens, awareness of surroundings.
- **Tailgating / piggybacking**: following someone through a secured door. Access control vestibules, badge checks.
- **Baiting**: leaving something tempting (infected USB drive) for a victim to pick up.
- **Quid pro quo**: offering a service or benefit in return for information or access.
- **Influence campaigns**: coordinated misinformation (often state-backed) to shape opinion.
- **Hoax**: a false warning (a fake virus alert) to cause panic or unneeded actions.

### Exam traps

- **Spear phishing** = targeted. **Whaling** = targeted at executives. **Vishing** = voice. **Smishing** = SMS.
- **Watering hole** hits a site the *targets use*, not the targets directly.
- **Typosquatting** = a deliberately mistyped domain. **Pharming** = DNS redirect with a correct URL typed.
- **Tailgating** can be unknowing (someone follows you through); **piggybacking** often has the authorized person's consent. The exam usually treats them together.
- **Default credentials** and **open ports** are configuration vectors, not software bugs.
- The attack surface shrinks when you remove unused services, ports, accounts and software.
