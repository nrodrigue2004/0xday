Resilience is the ability to keep working through failures and to recover quickly when something does break. This objective is about **availability**, one third of the CIA triad.

### High availability

- **Load balancing**: spreads work across multiple servers. Adds capacity and survives a server failure. Can be active/active.
- **Clustering**: servers work together as one system. **Active/passive**: a standby takes over on failure. **Active/active**: all nodes serve traffic.
- **Redundancy** at every layer: power supplies, NICs, disks (RAID), switches, internet links, data centers.
- **Multi-cloud** and **geographic dispersion** protect against a regional outage or provider problem.

### Site considerations (recovery sites)

| Site | State | Recovery time | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Hot site** | Fully equipped, data replicated, running | Minutes to hours | Highest |
| **Warm site** | Equipment ready, data restored when needed | Hours to days | Medium |
| **Cold site** | Empty space with power and connectivity | Days to weeks | Lowest |

- **Geographic dispersion**: put sites far enough apart that one disaster doesn't hit both.
- **Platform diversity**: using different operating systems, vendors or technologies so a single flaw can't take down everything.
- **Continuity of operations planning (COOP)**: how critical functions continue during a crisis.

### Capacity planning

Make sure you have enough of what the business needs. Plan for:

- **People**: staff to run operations (including during an incident).
- **Technology**: servers, storage, bandwidth, licenses.
- **Infrastructure**: space, power, cooling, network.

Under-capacity creates outages; elastic cloud capacity can scale to match demand.

### Recovery testing

You don't know a plan works until you test it.

- **Tabletop exercise**: a discussion-based walkthrough of a scenario. Cheap, low risk, finds gaps in plans and communication.
- **Failover test**: actually switch to the backup system or site to confirm it works.
- **Simulation**: a realistic drill that mimics an incident with participants acting it out.
- **Parallel processing**: run the recovery system alongside production to compare results without switching traffic.
- Record results, fix gaps, retest. Update the plan after every test and every real event.

### Backups

- **Types:** **full** (everything; slowest to take, fastest to restore), **incremental** (changes since the last backup of any type; fast to take, slowest to restore because you need the full plus every increment), **differential** (changes since the last *full*; restore needs the full plus the latest differential).
- **Snapshots**: point-in-time state of a VM or volume. Quick to take and revert, but usually on the same storage, so they are **not** a backup by themselves.
- **Replication**: copying data to another location continuously or on a schedule (synchronous for no data loss, asynchronous for distance).
- **Journaling**: records changes so state can be rebuilt after a failure.
- **Onsite vs offsite**: onsite restores faster; **offsite** (or cloud, or tape vaulted) survives a disaster at the main site. Follow the **3-2-1 rule**: 3 copies, 2 media types, 1 offsite (modern advice adds 1 offline/immutable).
- **Immutable and offline** backups resist ransomware that encrypts or deletes reachable backups.
- **Encryption** protects backups at rest and in transit; they hold all your data.
- **Frequency** is set by the **RPO** (recovery point objective): how much data you can afford to lose.
- **Retention** sets how long backups are kept; disposal must be secure.
- **Test restores** regularly. An untested backup is a hope, not a control.

### Power resiliency

- **Uninterruptible power supply (UPS)**: battery that bridges brief outages and allows safe shutdown; also conditions power (surges, sags).
- **Generator**: longer-term power, takes time to start, so it pairs with a UPS. Needs fuel and testing.
- **Dual power supplies and separate circuits/feeds** for critical equipment.
- **Managed PDUs** monitor and control power at the rack.

### Key metrics (also appear in 5.2)

| Metric | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **RTO** (recovery time objective) | Maximum acceptable time to restore a service |
| **RPO** (recovery point objective) | Maximum acceptable data loss, measured in time |
| **MTTR** (mean time to repair) | Average time to fix a failure |
| **MTBF** (mean time between failures) | Average time a repairable system runs between failures |

### More detail from the lessons

**High availability and sites**

- **Redundant** parts (spare in a box that you must rack and configure) is not HA. **HA** means the second system is already running beside the first and takes over load, at the cost of extra systems, better power and higher-quality components.
- **Server cluster**: several servers act as one (users see a single server), and members know about each other. Nodes can be added or removed in real time, usually with **identical operating systems**, and clustering is turned on in the OS. Nodes use **shared storage** rather than local disks to stay in sync.
- **Load balancing** differs: the servers do **not know about each other**, the central balancer decides who gets what, and servers can run different operating systems. It removes a failed server automatically and you can add capacity by adding servers.
- **Site resiliency**: a recovery site allocated ahead of time, with data synced, used for hours or weeks, then you move back. **Hot site** = exact replica of the data center (hardware bought twice, apps updated, data constantly synchronized). **Cold site** = empty building with power and lighting; you bring data, equipment and people. **Warm site** = in the middle: some equipment and some data, you add the rest.
- **Geographic dispersion**: a recovery site far away survives hurricanes or floods that would hit one down the street, but a farther site makes it harder to move equipment and staff (especially after a natural disaster) and to move back later.
- **Platform diversity**: vulnerabilities are usually OS-specific, so mix Linux and Windows in the data center and macOS and Windows on clients. **Multi-cloud**: AWS, Azure and Google Cloud have separate processes, so an outage or security concern at one provider need not affect another.
- **COOP (continuity of operations planning)**: when technology is unavailable, use non-technical fallbacks: manual signed transactions, paper receipts, phoning in a card for approval. Design these beforehand.

**Capacity planning**

- Too little supply gives slowdowns and outages; too much wastes money. Plan **people** (call center staff are hard to ramp up or down; hiring and training takes time and money), **technology** (choose things that scale, such as load-balanced web servers, multiple SQL servers, or splitting databases across servers), and **infrastructure**.
- The cloud makes deployment easy (no hardware to buy, rack and test): click to create an instance, ramp up in busy periods, then **rightsize** when demand drops. More cloud resources means higher bills.

**Recovery testing**

- Run recovery tests on a regular schedule with a specific scope and time window that does not affect production, then evaluate and update the plan. Full tests are expensive, so use a **tabletop exercise** (talk through the steps around a table with other departments) to find logistics gaps.
- **Failover test**: ideally automatic and invisible to users. Needs redundant switches, firewalls and routers (many have failover built in). Example design: two ISPs, two routers, redundant firewalls, multiple switches, multiple server links, and load balancers.
- **Simulations**: phishing simulation (can the systems detect it, who clicked, who needs more training), password reset process tests, data exfiltration tests.
- **Parallel processing**: spread transactions across multiple cores or computers for efficiency, and if one processor is lost the rest absorb the load.

**Backups in detail**

- Decide: how much data, the backup type, media (tape, disk, cloud), where to store it (on site, off site, cloud), the software, and the schedule (full daily, or daily changes with a weekly full).
- **On-site**: same location, no WAN needed, immediately available, cheaper. **Off-site**: transfer or ship media elsewhere, reachable from a new data center after a disaster. Many organizations use both (on-site for fast restores, off-site for long term).
- Frequency depends on data volume and how often it changes. Multiple **backup sets**: for example about 30 daily, 4 weekly and 12 monthly copies.
- **Encrypt backups**, especially in the cloud. Story: tapes stolen from an employee's car during an errand on the way to the storage facility. Keep the **recovery keys** safe so you can restore.
- **Snapshots**: one click for a VM or cloud system, similar to an incremental backup (changes only). Lesson example: a 1 TB VM with 100 GB used; Monday snapshot captures all 100 GB; Tuesday has 40 GB changed; Wednesday grows to 120 GB with 20 GB new. Take one daily and before big changes, and revert in a few clicks.
- **Test your restores** regularly by simulating a disaster or restoring one database, then confirm **applications can use the data** (that is the second half of the test).
- **Replication**: copy one source to one or many sites in near real time (seconds or minutes); the copy is used for backup storage, a replicated infrastructure or a **hot site** disaster recovery.
- **Journaling**: write to a journal on the drive first, then copy to the final location. A power loss during the journal write loses only the journal; a loss between journal and database is repaired from the journal on startup. After success, clear the journal.

**Power in detail**

- Power outages can last minutes, hours or days. A **UPS** covers blackouts, **brownouts** (low voltage) and **surges** (excess voltage). Types: **offline/standby** (runs on mains, switches to battery on failure; low end), **line-interactive** (can raise voltage during brownouts), and **online/double-conversion** (always runs from the battery, no switching). Size the batteries for the time you need. Many have signaling to shut systems down **gracefully** before the battery empties, plus outlet counts and surge suppression for Ethernet and phone lines.
- **Generator**: long-term power while there is fuel; may power a whole building or marked outlets only. There is a **start-up ramp of about a minute**, so use both: UPS covers the gap until the generator takes over.

### Exam traps

- **Hot > warm > cold** in speed and cost.
- **Differential** needs the last full + latest differential; **incremental** needs the last full + all increments.
- Snapshots and replication are not substitutes for backups (replication copies corruption and deletions too).
- **RPO** drives backup frequency; **RTO** drives how fast the recovery solution must be.
- A **tabletop** is discussion only; a **failover** really switches systems.
