You inherit the risks of everyone you do business with. This objective covers how to assess vendors and the agreements that bind them.

### Third-party risk assessment

- **Vendor assessment**: judge suppliers before and during the relationship.
  - **Penetration testing** (with permission) and **right-to-audit clauses** allow verification.
  - **Evidence of internal audits** and **independent assessments** (SOC 2 reports, ISO 27001 certification).
  - **Supply chain analysis**: who are *their* suppliers? (fourth parties)
  - **Vendor questionnaires** about security practices, incident history and controls.
- **Vendor selection**: **due diligence** (investigate before signing), **conflict of interest** check.
- **Vendor monitoring**: continuous review of performance, security posture and incidents. Reassess periodically.
- **Vendor viability**, **financial stability**, and **geographic location** (laws and data sovereignty).
- **Questionnaires**, **rules of engagement** for tests, and **right to audit** are standard.
- **Offboarding / termination**: return or destroy data, revoke access, confirm deletion.

### Agreement types

| Agreement | Purpose | Key features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **SLA** (service level agreement) | Defines the level of service a provider will deliver | Measurable targets such as 99.9% uptime, response times, penalties |
| **MOA** (memorandum of agreement) | Formal agreement on how parties will work together | Stronger than an MOU; sets responsibilities |
| **MOU** (memorandum of understanding) | Statement of shared intent | Generally non-binding; informal |
| **MSA** (master service agreement) | Umbrella contract with standing terms | Later work orders reference it, speeding up new projects |
| **WO / SOW** (work order / statement of work) | Defines a specific project | Scope, deliverables, deadlines, acceptance criteria, cost |
| **NDA** (non-disclosure agreement) | Keeps information confidential | Legal consequences for sharing; mutual or one-way |
| **BPA** (business partners agreement) | Sets how partners operate together | Profit/loss sharing, responsibilities, exit |

Contracts often add **security requirements**, **breach notification timelines**, **audit rights**, **data handling** and **termination clauses**.

### More detail from the lessons

- **Why assess vendors**: a payroll provider holds a great deal of your data, so put the risk assessment terms in the contract, with penalties for breach.
- **Penetration testing** a vendor: similar to a vulnerability scan but actively exploits. It can be required at standard intervals and run by a specialist third party. The **rules of engagement** state the test type (on-site physical, internal, or across the internet), dates and hours (business or after hours), IP ranges, emergency contacts, handling of sensitive data found, and which devices are in scope and out of scope.
- **Right to audit** clause: audits happen regularly, can set how they run, and are often performed by an outside party who is not part of the contract. Audit focus: access management, offboarding, password handling and storage, VPN access controls. Repeat audits over a long relationship.
- **Supply chain analysis** follows the chain from raw materials to final product: how the product reaches the customer, how groups coordinate, how security is handled between the two technical teams, and changes to business process. **SolarWinds**: malware slipped into its product code and shipped with a valid digital signature between March and June 2020, announced December 2020; of roughly 300,000 customers, at least 18,000 installed it.
- **Independent assessments** give an outside view built on experience across many organizations.
- **Due diligence** means verifying claims (revenue, customer count) and can include background checks and interviews. **Conflict of interest** examples: the vendor also serves your biggest competitor, employs an executive's relative, or offers gifts for signing.
- **Monitoring** after signing: financial health checks, IT security reviews, news and social media. Someone in the company owns each vendor relationship, and monitoring should be both quantitative and qualitative. **Questionnaires** ask about the vendor's due diligence, conflict handling, disaster recovery and data storage/protection; answers feed an ongoing vendor risk analysis.
- **SLA** example for an ISP: no more than four hours unscheduled downtime, technician dispatch, spare equipment kept on site.
- **MOU**: broad goals, may include confidentiality, informal and unsigned in spirit. **MOA**: a step above, more detail on the relationship, may hold some binding language, but is not a contract and is generally not legally enforceable.
- **MSA** is a legal contract with terms (service, billing, payment) that later **work orders / SOWs** build on. A **SOW** lists scope, location, deliverables schedule and expectations, and settles disputes over what was or was not covered.
- **NDA** forms: **unilateral** (one party bound), **bilateral/mutual** (both), **multilateral** (more than two). It is a formal signed contract, signed before sharing the protected information.
- **BPA**: financial details, ownership stake, who makes business decisions, and what happens when finances go wrong or a disaster closes the business.

### Exam traps

- **SLA** is about *service levels*; **SOW** is about *project scope*; **MSA** is the *master* terms.
- **MOU** is informal and non-binding; **MOA** is more detailed but still generally not an enforceable contract; **MSA** and **NDA** are legal contracts.
- **NDA** protects confidentiality.
- Third-party risk includes **fourth parties** (your vendor's vendors).
- You can outsource work, not accountability.
