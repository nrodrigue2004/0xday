A **threat actor** is whoever (or whatever) causes a security event. The exam asks you to identify an actor from a scenario using four clues: where they are (internal or external), how much money and tooling they have, how sophisticated they are, and **why** they are attacking.

### Attributes used to describe a threat actor

- **Internal vs external**: inside the organization (employee, contractor) or outside.
- **Resources and funding**: from almost nothing to the budget of a government.
- **Level of sophistication and capability**: running someone else's script, building custom tools, or developing zero-days.
- **Motivation**: the reason behind the attack. This is the most testable attribute.

### Motivations to know

Data exfiltration, **espionage**, **service disruption**, **blackmail**, **financial gain**, **philosophical/political beliefs**, **ethical** (e.g. a researcher disclosing a flaw), **revenge**, **disruption/chaos**, and **war**.

### The actors

| Actor | Location | Resources | Sophistication | Typical motivation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Nation state** | External | Enormous | Very high | Espionage, data theft, political and philosophical goals, disruption, war |
| **Unskilled attacker** ("script kiddie") | Usually external, can be internal | Very limited | Very low | Disruption, curiosity, notoriety, using ready-made tools |
| **Hacktivist** | Usually external | Limited, sometimes crowdfunded | Can be high | Philosophical or political: defacement, DoS, leaking documents |
| **Insider threat** | Internal | Already has access | Medium (knows where data is and how to dodge controls) | Revenge, financial gain, sometimes accidental |
| **Organized crime** | External | Large, run like a business | High | Money: ransomware, fraud, selling stolen data |
| **Shadow IT** | Internal | Small departmental budget | Low | Convenience and speed, bypassing IT rules (not malicious by intent) |

### Details on each

- **Nation state**: usually behind an **advanced persistent threat (APT)**: a patient, long-term intrusion. Targets include military, utilities, finance and defense contractors. Stuxnet (reported US/Israel) is the classic example: a worm built to destroy nuclear centrifuges.
- **Unskilled attacker**: runs downloaded scripts without understanding them. If the script fails, they cannot fix it. Looks for the easiest way in.
- **Hacktivist**: hacker plus activist. Defaces websites, runs DoS, leaks private documents to push a cause. May even take a job to become an insider.
- **Insider threat**: employees, contractors or partners with legitimate access. Harder to detect because activity looks normal. Defenses: vetting at hiring, least privilege, separation of duties, monitoring, offboarding.
- **Organized crime**: structured like a company with specialists (developers, access brokers, negotiators, even customer support for ransomware victims). Profit-driven.
- **Shadow IT**: a department that builds or buys its own systems and cloud services (often with a credit card) outside IT's change control, backups and security review. Not a malicious attacker, but it creates unmanaged risk.

> Exam trick: scenarios often hide the motivation in a sentence. "Posted internal documents to protest the company's policies" is hacktivist. "Left after being passed over and wiped the shared drive" is insider with a revenge motive. "Demanded cryptocurrency" is organized crime.

### Why motivation matters for defense

Knowing the likely actor changes your controls: an insider needs least privilege and monitoring; organized crime needs offline backups and phishing resistance; a nation state may need segmentation, threat hunting and supply chain scrutiny; shadow IT needs governance, approved alternatives and cloud discovery.

### More detail from the lessons

- Knowing the actor helps explain why an attack is happening and what the goal was. Attributes: inside or outside, funding, sophistication (from running a script blindly to building your own tools) and motivation, which varies with the attacker and the victim (a competitor spying, or someone just disrupting service).
- **Nation state**: an entire government or its national security arm. Motivations include data exfiltration, philosophical or political reasons, disruption and pulling someone into war. It can run constant attacks on many locations at once (APTs), with the best developers, against military control sites, utilities and another country's finances. Stuxnet was reportedly built jointly by the United States and Israel to destroy nuclear centrifuges.
- **Unskilled attacker**: success only if the script works, and no ability to fix it if it fails. Motivated by the attack itself, whether disruption, data theft or a political cause. Usually outside but sometimes inside. Looks for the easiest path using widely available scripts.
- **Hacktivist**: technically strong; favors DoS, defacement and leaking private documents. Usually outside, but may get hired to become an internal threat. Funds are small, though some groups fundraise.
- **Insider threat**: more than someone leaving a password on a sticky note. It is revenge or financial gain, using resources that already exist. Proper vetting at hiring matters. Medium sophistication, but they know where the data is and how to get around controls.
- **Organized crime**: profit is the motive, and it has corporate structure: one person hacks, another manages and writes exploits, another sells the data, and another handles customer support in ransomware cases.
- **Shadow IT**: a group or department going around IT, building its own infrastructure and using its own budget or credit cards for cloud services reached from a browser. It escapes change control and budgeting, and often lacks IT background, so backups, change control and security get no thought.
- **Summary table**: nation state, unskilled, hacktivist and organized crime are typically external; insider and shadow IT are internal. Nation states and organized crime are highly sophisticated; unskilled attackers and shadow IT have low sophistication. Once you know the motivation you can adjust defenses.

### Also on the exam: threat actor categories by skill

- **Script kiddie**: low skill, runs others' tools.
- **Hacker types**: **white hat** (authorized, ethical), **black hat** (malicious), **gray hat** (unauthorized but not malicious, may disclose publicly).
