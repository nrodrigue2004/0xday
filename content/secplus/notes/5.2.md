Risk management is about deciding which risks to treat, how, and in what order. The exam mixes definitions, formulas and strategies.

### Core ideas

- **Risk** = likelihood × impact: the chance that a **threat** exploits a **vulnerability** and the harm that follows.
- **Risk assessment** identifies and evaluates risks. **Risk register** lists identified risks, owners, ratings, and treatment. **Risk owner** is the person accountable for a specific risk. **Risk tolerance / appetite** is how much risk the organization will accept. **Risk threshold** is the line that triggers action.
- **Inherent risk** is before controls; **residual risk** is what is left after controls; **control risk** is the chance controls fail.
- **Risk reporting** communicates risks to leadership. **Risk indicators** (KRIs) warn that risk is rising.
- **Risk posture** is the overall state of risk. **Risk appetite types**: expansionary (accepts more), conservative (accepts little), neutral.

### Risk assessment types

| Type | When |
| --- | --- |
| **Ad hoc** | One-off, as needed |
| **Recurring** | On a schedule (annual) |
| **One-time** | For a specific project or change |
| **Continuous** | Ongoing monitoring |

### Qualitative vs quantitative analysis

- **Qualitative**: ranks risk with descriptive scales (low, medium, high) using judgment and **risk matrices**/heat maps. Quick, subjective.
- **Quantitative**: uses numbers and money.

**Quantitative formulas (memorize):**

| Term | Meaning | Formula |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **AV** | Asset value | |
| **EF** | Exposure factor: percent of the asset lost in one event | |
| **SLE** | Single loss expectancy: cost of one event | **SLE = AV × EF** |
| **ARO** | Annualized rate of occurrence: how many times per year | |
| **ALE** | Annualized loss expectancy: expected yearly loss | **ALE = SLE × ARO** |

**Example:** a server worth $100,000 would lose 50% of its value in a flood (EF 0.5): SLE = $50,000. Floods are expected once every 10 years (ARO 0.1): ALE = $5,000. A mitigation costing more than $5,000 per year is not cost-effective for this risk.

Other terms: **probability**, **likelihood**, **impact**, **exposure factor**. **Vulnerability** and **threat** feed the likelihood.

### Risk management strategies

| Strategy | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Avoid** | Stop the risky activity | Don't launch the risky service |
| **Mitigate** | Reduce likelihood or impact with controls | Patch, segment, train |
| **Transfer** | Shift financial impact to another party | Cyber insurance, outsourcing |
| **Accept** | Live with it | Exception/exemption with documented approval; **risk acceptance** |
| **Share** (sometimes listed) | Spread between parties | Joint ventures |

- **Exemptions/exceptions** are formal acceptance with documentation and expiry.
- You can transfer financial loss, but not accountability.
- Controls should cost less than the risk they reduce.

### Risk register and reporting

A register lists each risk, description, owner, likelihood, impact, rating, controls, and status. It's reviewed regularly and drives treatment plans and reporting to executives.

### Business impact analysis (BIA)

The BIA identifies **critical functions**, the systems they depend on, and what happens when they are unavailable. It produces the recovery metrics:

| Metric | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **RTO** | Recovery time objective: maximum tolerable downtime before the function must be restored |
| **RPO** | Recovery point objective: maximum tolerable data loss, as time since last good copy |
| **MTTR** | Mean time to repair: average time to fix |
| **MTBF** | Mean time between failures: reliability of repairable systems |
| **MTTF** | Mean time to failure (non-repairable items) |
| **Recovery site / mission-essential functions / identification of critical systems / single points of failure** | What to protect first, what has no redundancy |

BIA feeds business continuity and disaster recovery planning.

### More detail from the lessons

- **Assessment timing**: **one-time** assessments tie to a project (an acquisition, new equipment or software). **Ad hoc** ones answer a specific question, for example the CEO hears of an attack type at a conference and a temporary committee studies only that, then disbands. **Recurring** ones run every quarter, six months or year, often internally; PCI DSS requires an annual one for card-holding firms. **Continuous** assessment is built into change control, where each change gets a risk step.
- **Qualitative example** (traffic-light grid scoring impact, ARO, cost of controls, overall risk): legacy Windows clients (medium impact, high ARO, medium cost) rate high overall; untrained staff (low impact, medium ARO, low cost) rate medium; devices with no antivirus (medium impact, high ARO, medium cost) rate very high.
- **Asset value** is not just replacement cost; it can include lost sales and fines. **EF** is 0.25 for a quarter lost and 1.0 for total loss. **Laptop example**: AV $1,000, EF 1.0, so SLE $1,000; seven stolen per year gives ALE $7,000. The data on the laptop may be worth more than the laptop, which is why qualitative analysis is still needed.
- **Impact categories**: life first (people cannot be replaced), then property, safety and finance.
- **Likelihood** is qualitative (rare, possible, almost certain); **probability** is quantitative and based on history or expectation. The terms are often used interchangeably.
- **Appetite vs tolerance**: appetite is how much risk the organization is willing to take, and its qualitative stance is the **risk appetite posture** (conservative, neutral, expansionary). Tolerance is the wider band around it. Speed-limit analogy: the limit is the appetite; police not ticketing until well over it is the tolerance, which tightens in bad weather.
- **Risk register**: each line has a key risk indicator (for example purpose not well defined, deliverables incomplete, schedule unclear), an **owner**, and a **threshold**, balancing money spent against what the risk would cost.
- **Strategies**: **transfer** (cyber insurance), **accept** (the most common), **exemption** (a policy cannot be followed: a manufacturing machine running unpatchable Windows is exempted if kept off the network), **exception** (policy says patch within three days, but the patch crashes a critical app so the deadline is extended), **avoid** (remove the risk entirely), **mitigate** (a next-generation firewall for internet exposure). **Risk reporting** lists critical and emerging risks for management purchase decisions and is constantly updated.
- **BIA metrics**: **RTO** is time until operational (for example both the database and web server running). **RPO** is the data that must be restored before you count as operational (for example the last 12 months of customer data). **MTTR** covers diagnosing, getting, installing and configuring replacement gear; a two-hour third-party replacement contract or on-site spares lower it. **MTBF** is estimated time between outages, from the manufacturer or history; roughly total uptime divided by number of breakdowns.

### Exam traps

- **SLE × ARO = ALE**; **AV × EF = SLE**.
- Insurance = **transfer**. Policy exception = **accept**. Firewall rule = **mitigate**. Shutting the service = **avoid**.
- **Residual risk** remains after controls; you accept it or add more controls.
- **RPO** is about data loss; **RTO** is about time to restore service.
- **Qualitative** uses categories; **quantitative** uses numbers.
