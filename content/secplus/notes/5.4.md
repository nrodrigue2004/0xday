Compliance means following the laws, regulations and standards that apply to you, and being able to prove it. Failing has consequences.

### Compliance reporting and monitoring

- **Internal** compliance reporting: for management, audit committees, and governance. **External** reporting: to regulators, auditors, customers.
- **Compliance monitoring**: continuous checks that controls operate as required. Includes **due diligence / due care** (research before acting; acting responsibly), **attestation and acknowledgement** (people or systems formally confirm compliance), and **automation** (compliance scanning, evidence collection).

### Consequences of non-compliance

- **Fines** and **sanctions** from regulators.
- **Reputational damage** and loss of customers.
- **Loss of license** to operate.
- **Contractual impacts** (lost business, penalties).
- **Criminal** liability in serious cases.

### Privacy

- **Legal implications**: local, regional, national and global laws apply, depending on where the data subjects live.
- **Data subject** rights: access, correction, deletion, portability, objection.
- **Controller** decides purpose; **processor** acts on its behalf; **data protection officer** oversees compliance.
- **Right to be forgotten**, **consent**, **data minimization**, **retention limits**, **breach notification**.
- **Data inventory and retention** policies determine what you hold and how long.
- **Privacy impact assessment** evaluates risks of a project that handles personal data.
- **Sovereignty** and cross-border transfer rules.

### More detail from the lessons

- **Compliance** means meeting standards set by law or by agreement with a third party. Penalties include fines, job loss and even prison. A **central compliance officer (CCO)** makes sure the organization follows state, local, federal and other requirements and reports the status. External requirements need periodic reports; wrong or missed reports bring penalties or sanctions.
- **Laws named**: **SOX** (Public Company Accounting Reform and Investor Protection Act, 2002), **HIPAA** (medical privacy in the US) and **GLBA** (Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act, 1999; the privacy notices from your bank).
- **HIPAA penalty tiers** (as given in the lesson): up to $50,000 and/or one year for a basic violation; up to $100,000 and/or five years if done under false pretenses; up to $250,000 and/or ten years if the intent is commercial advantage, personal gain or malicious harm; civil fines of $100 per violation capped at $25,000 for identical violations. Exact figures are not what the exam tests; know that penalties scale with intent.
- **Reputational damage example**: Uber was breached in October 2016 (25.6 million names, emails and phone numbers), disclosed it only in November 2017 after allegedly paying the hackers $100,000 under an NDA, paid $148 million in 2018, and its former security chief got three years' probation and a $50,000 fine in May 2023. Disclosing on time would have kept it compliant.
- **Other consequences**: loss of a license needed to sell the product, sanctions that stop others buying from you, and contractual breach, which two private parties can often settle without legal proceedings.
- **Due care** is what you do internally; **due diligence** is what you do with third parties. The responsible executive signs the **attestation and acknowledgment** that compliance is in good standing. Large firms automate monitoring with tools that collect data from people, third parties and other departments and compile reports.
- **Privacy** starts locally (property, vehicle and medical licensing records), then national (HIPAA), then multinational (**GDPR** for everyone living in the EU). Protected data includes name, address, photo, email, bank details, social posts, genetic makeup and location. The **right to be forgotten** lets a **data subject** demand removal; a data subject is any identified or identifiable natural person, so effectively all of us. GDPR frames privacy from the individual's perspective rather than the company's.
- **Payroll example**: the payroll department is the controller (decides pay and timing) and the outside payroll company is the processor; an NDA keeps the exchanged data private. A processor can be internal or a third party.
- **Data inventory**: a list of all data held, with owner, update frequency and format. Know the uses (collaboration, IT security, data quality checks) and check that any sharing with outsiders fits privacy law.

### Exam traps

- **GDPR** covers EU residents' data wherever the company is.
- **PCI DSS** is an industry standard, not a law; **HIPAA** is law.
- **Due diligence** = investigate before; **due care** = act reasonably after.
- **Attestation** is a formal confirmation of compliance.
- Compliance is **not** the same as security, but it sets a minimum.
