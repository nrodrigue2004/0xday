People are both the weakest link and the best sensor. Awareness practices turn staff into a defense layer, with training aimed at specific risks.

### Phishing awareness

- **Recognizing phishing**: sender mismatch, urgency, unexpected attachments, odd links, requests for credentials or money. Hover to preview links; verify through another channel.
- **Phishing campaigns and simulations**: send test phishing emails to measure who clicks and train those who do, with no shaming. Track **click rate** and **report rate** over time.
- **Reporting**: an easy "report phish" button and a safe, blame-free process.
- **Responding to suspicious messages**: do not click, report, delete.

### Anomalous behavior recognition

Teach users to notice and report:

- **Risky behavior** (sharing passwords, tailgating, plugging in unknown USB drives, ignoring warnings).
- **Unexpected behavior** (strange pop-ups, devices acting oddly, odd account activity).
- **Unintentional** behavior (accidental data sharing, misdirected email) versus intentional.
- Signs of **insider threats** (unusual access, copying large data volumes).

### Awareness delivery and content

- **User guidance and training** on policies, handling data, passwords and MFA, social engineering, safe browsing, removable media, remote work and travel security.
- **Situational awareness**: know your surroundings (shoulder surfers, public Wi-Fi, device left unattended).
- **Insider threat** training, **password management**, **removable media**, **social engineering**, **operational security**, **hybrid/remote work environments**.
- **Reporting channels** so employees know who to contact.

### Training program practices

- **Development**: align content with real risks and roles; role-based training for admins, executives, developers and HR.
- **Execution**: new-hire training, **periodic refreshers**, **recurring** reminders, posters, newsletters, lunch-and-learns, gamification.
- **Reporting and monitoring**: **initial** and **recurring** training records, completion rates, quizzes, phishing results.
- **Effectiveness** measured and adjusted; training must be **engaging and relevant**.
- **Policy/handbook awareness**: acceptable use acknowledgement.
- **Culture**: leadership participation, positive reinforcement, encouraging reporting over blame.

### More detail from the lessons

- **Phishing campaigns** can be built internally or bought. An automated console reports opens, clicks and interactions; a user who clicks gets an automated message and is sent to online or in-person training.
- **What users should check**: spelling and grammar errors, the link's domain name, inconsistencies in how the message is built, unusual attachments and requests for personal information or credentials. Never click links or run attachments from email. The email filter should stop most of it, so incoming phishing also tests the filter. Users need a well-known path to report suspected phishing to IT security.
- **Anomalous behavior** types: **risky** (modifying the hosts file, replacing a core OS file, uploading sensitive files), **unexpected** (login from another country, a jump in data transfers) and **unintentional** (typing the wrong domain, losing a USB drive, a misconfigured security setting).
- **Automated reporting** of daily metrics: phishing click rate, password manager adoption, MFA use. First offense gets training; repeat events flag users for extended training or tighter configuration.
- The **security awareness team** monitors, reports and trains: emails, posters, classroom sessions, role-based content, and training for specific compliance mandates. Metrics are tracked over time and shown to stakeholders.
- **User training** should come before first network connection and be specialized by department (accounting vs shipping). It covers contractors, partners and suppliers too, and training records show who is done. Policies belong on the intranet and in the employee handbook.
- **Situational awareness**: phishing by email, odd URLs and text messages, and physical attacks such as an official-looking envelope containing a USB drive.
- **Insider threat** defenses: multiple approvals for critical process changes, active file monitoring with immediate alerts, and making bypass hard.
- **Password management** guidance (length, complexity) can be enforced by Windows group policy. Training also covers unknown USB drives and public charging cables, social engineering, operational security (thinking like the attacker, knowing what data is sensitive), and **remote work**: no access for family or friends, extra endpoint protection and stronger VPN security.

### Exam traps

- Simulated phishing is **training**, not punishment.
- **Reporting** matters as much as avoiding; a report gives early warning.
- Training should be **role-based** and **recurring**, not just once at hire.
- **Operational** controls (see 1.1) include awareness programs.
