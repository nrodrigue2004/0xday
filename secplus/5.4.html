5.4 Summarize elements of effective security compliance

key ideas

  • Compliance reporting (internal, external). Consequences of non-compliance: fines, sanctions, reputational damage, loss of license, contractual impacts.
  • Compliance monitoring: due diligence/care, attestation and acknowledgement, internal/external, automation.
  • Privacy: legal implications, data subject, controller vs processor, ownership, data inventory and retention, right to be forgotten.

study notes

Compliance means following the laws, regulations and standards that apply to you, and being able to prove it. Failing has consequences.

Compliance reporting and monitoring

  • Internal compliance reporting: for management, audit committees, and governance. External reporting: to regulators, auditors, customers.
  • Compliance monitoring: continuous checks that controls operate as required. Includes due diligence / due care (research before acting; acting responsibly), attestation and acknowledgement (people or systems formally confirm compliance), and automation (compliance scanning, evidence collection).

Consequences of non-compliance

  • Fines and sanctions from regulators.
  • Reputational damage and loss of customers.
  • Loss of license to operate.
  • Contractual impacts (lost business, penalties).
  • Criminal liability in serious cases.

Privacy

  • Legal implications: local, regional, national and global laws apply, depending on where the data subjects live.
  • Data subject rights: access, correction, deletion, portability, objection.
  • Controller decides purpose; processor acts on its behalf; data protection officer oversees compliance.
  • Right to be forgotten, consent, data minimization, retention limits, breach notification.
  • Data inventory and retention policies determine what you hold and how long.
  • Privacy impact assessment evaluates risks of a project that handles personal data.
  • Sovereignty and cross-border transfer rules.

Exam traps

  • GDPR covers EU residents' data wherever the company is.
  • PCI DSS is an industry standard, not a law; HIPAA is law.
  • Due diligence = investigate before; due care = act reasonably after.
  • Attestation is a formal confirmation of compliance.
  • Compliance is not the same as security, but it sets a minimum.
