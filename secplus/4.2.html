SY0-701domain 4.0 · Security Operations4.2

4.2 Explain the security implications of proper hardware, software, and data asset management

key ideas

  • Acquisition/procurement, assignment/accounting (ownership, classification), monitoring/asset tracking (inventory, enumeration).
  • Disposal/decommissioning: sanitization, destruction, certification, data retention.

study notes

You cannot protect what you don't know you have. Asset management covers the whole life of hardware, software and data, from buying through disposal.

The asset life cycle

StageWhat happensSecurity concern
Acquisition / procurementRequesting, approving and buyingBuy from trusted sources to avoid counterfeit or tampered products; define security requirements; vendor due diligence
Assignment / accountingGive it an owner and record itOwnership and classification of each asset; someone is accountable for it
Monitoring / asset trackingKeep the inventory accurateKnow what exists, where, its patch level and its status
Disposal / decommissioningRetire it safelyData must be removed or destroyed; keep records

Inventory and enumeration

  • Inventory: a list of all assets with details (type, owner, location, serial number, OS version, software, classification, purchase and warranty dates). An asset you don't know about is an asset you can't patch.
  • Enumeration: discovering what is on the network, with scanning and network access control, so unknown devices (shadow IT) show up.
  • Tracking tools: barcode or RFID tags, asset management software, configuration management database (CMDB), mobile device management.
  • Track software licenses and approved software lists too.

Ownership and classification

Every asset has a business owner who decides how it's used and what protection it needs, and is classified (public, private, confidential, restricted) so controls match its value (see 3.3).

Disposal and decommissioning

When hardware or media is retired, data may still be recoverable. Pick the method based on data sensitivity and whether the device will be reused.

MethodWhat it doesReusable afterward?
Sanitization (wiping)Overwrites data (secure erase, multiple-pass, cryptographic erase) so it can't be recovered. Follows NIST SP 800-88Yes
Cryptographic eraseDestroy the encryption key so encrypted data is unreadableYes
DegaussingStrong magnetic field scrambles data on magnetic media (HDD, tape)HDD no longer works; no effect on SSD
Physical destructionShredding, pulverizing, drilling, incineration, disintegratingNo
Certificate of destructionA vendor's written proof of destructionn/a
  • Retention: keep data only as long as policy or law requires, then dispose of it. Over-retention increases breach impact; under-retention can break legal requirements.
  • Legal hold: when litigation is possible, relevant data must be preserved and *not* disposed.
  • Media types: SSDs and flash need different methods than magnetic disks (degaussing doesn't work on SSD). Paper needs shredding or pulping.
  • Document the disposal: who, what, when, how, and keep the certificate.

Exam traps

  • Degaussing only works on magnetic media.
  • Sanitization lets you reuse the device; destruction does not.
  • Cryptographic erase is the fast option for encrypted drives.
  • Every asset needs an owner and a classification.
  • Don't dispose of data under legal hold.
