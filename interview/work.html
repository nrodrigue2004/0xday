# what the resume says

  • Performed secure data sanitization with XErase and EPSBoot to ensure compliance before resale
  • Diagnosed and repaired hardware and software issues across many laptop models, cutting device-to-resale turnaround
  • Installed and configured Windows, drivers and software for refurbished laptops
  • Evaluated and refined LLM-generated outputs, giving structured feedback to improve accuracy and response quality

Every bullet is a door. An interviewer picks one and asks "tell me more", so you need a story behind each.

# the pitch

30-second version. "As a laptop technician I sanitize drives securely before resale, diagnose and repair hardware and software faults across many models, and install Windows, drivers and software. I also work as an AI trainer, where I evaluate language model output and give structured feedback."

What the interviewer is testing: reliability, process, and attention to detail. Numbers help: how many laptops, how long per unit, what you cut.

  • Fill in real numbers (units per week, turnaround) before the interview. Do not invent them.

# questions // 6

Model answers show the shape. Replace the details with what you actually did: a borrowed story falls apart at the first follow-up. Generic questions are in interview basics, role-specific ones in ~/careers.

  1. [easy] What does a day as a laptop technician look like?
    Intake and inspection, diagnosis, repair or part swap, sanitize the drive, install the OS and drivers, test, document. Add your own details.
  2. [easy] Why does secure data sanitization matter?
    Resold devices can leak the previous owner's data if the drive isn't properly wiped. Compliance and customer trust depend on it, and a wipe should be verified, not assumed.
  3. [normal] How do you diagnose a laptop that won't boot?
    Power and charger first, then listen and look (lights, beeps), reseat RAM and drive, try a known-good part, check BIOS detection of the drive, boot from external media. Isolate hardware versus software by what changes.
  4. [normal] How did you reduce turnaround time?
    Describe the actual change: a standard imaging process, checklists, batching, keeping common parts on hand. Quantify only if you measured it.
  5. [normal] What does an AI trainer do, and what skills transfer to security?
    Reading carefully, finding factual and logical errors, and writing precise feedback against criteria. That maps to triage, analysis and report writing. Don't reveal confidential details of the work.
  6. [hard] A customer insists their data was left on a device you sanitized. What do you do?
    Take it seriously: pull the record of the wipe method and verification for that unit, inspect the device if possible, escalate to a lead, and report the finding honestly. Document and fix the process if there was a gap.
