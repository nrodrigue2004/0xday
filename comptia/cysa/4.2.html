CS0-004domain 4.0 · Reporting and Communication4.2

4.2 Explain the importance of security operations and incident response reporting and communication

study notes

  • Declaration and escalation: define who can declare an incident, on what criteria, and who gets notified at each severity.
  • Executive summary: one page: what happened, impact, current status, decisions needed. No jargon.
  • Communication plan: stakeholders include legal (privilege, breach laws), PR (public statements), regulators (GDPR 72 hours, HIPAA, SEC and others), law enforcement, customers, employees. One spokesperson, no speculation, no blame. Use secure channels and know your role.
  • Operational awareness: choose channels (ticket, chat, phone, out-of-band) and keep sensitive details to the need-to-know group.
  • Post-incident: after-action report (timeline, actions, what worked), lessons learned (meeting soon after, blameless), RCA.
  • Handover: shift notes: open incidents, pending actions, key contacts, so nothing is dropped.
  • Internal threat intel report: tailored to your environment and adversaries.
  • Metrics: alert volume, false-positive rate, true-positive rate, MTTD (detect), MTTR (respond or remediate), mean time to close, phishing click rate.

flashcards

self-check

1. Who should speak to the press during an incident?

  • a) Any analyst
  • b) Designated spokesperson via PR/legal
  • c) The attacker
  • d) Helpdesk
answer

b) A single approved voice avoids leaks and contradictions.

2. Which metric would drop if phishing training works?

  • a) MTTD
  • b) Phishing click rate
  • c) Alert volume
  • d) CPU usage
answer

b) Fewer users clicking simulated phish.

short lab: Handover note

Write a 5-line shift handover for an open phishing incident.

walkthrough

Include: incident ID and severity; current status (e.g., 12 mailboxes purged, 2 users pending); pending actions with owners; key contacts and approvals; next checkpoint time.
