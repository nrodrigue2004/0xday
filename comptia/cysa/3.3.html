CS0-004domain 3.0 · Incident Response and Management3.3

3.3 Given a scenario, implement incident response techniques

study notes

Prepare

  • IR plan (policy, roles, severity definitions, escalation) and communication plan (who is told what, when, by whom, out-of-band channels in case email is compromised). Playbooks per scenario. Roles: incident commander, analysts, comms/legal/PR, system owners. Training: tabletop (talk through a scenario) and simulation (hands-on exercise).

Investigate

  • Log collection, correlation (join events across sources), augmentation and enrichment (add reputation, asset owner, user). Alerts and notifications feed triage: is it real, how urgent.
  • Build the timeline (first malicious event to now) and determine severity and impact (data types, systems, users, business function). Prioritize by impact and spread.

Evidence

  • Chain of custody: who held the evidence, when, where, with signatures. Integrity validation: hash (SHA-256) before and after copying. Preservation: write blockers, forensic images, store securely. Legal hold: suspend deletion of data that might be needed in litigation.

Act

  • Isolate affected targets (EDR network contain, VLAN, disable port). Escalate per severity. Remediate and verify (patch, rebuild, rescan, monitor). Release from isolation only after verification. Restore from known-good backups.
  • Root cause analysis asks how it got in and why defenses missed it; corrective actions assign owners and deadlines.

flashcards

self-check

1. You image a disk for investigation. What proves the copy matches the original?

  • a) File size
  • b) Matching SHA-256 hashes
  • c) Same filename
  • d) Timestamps
answer

b) Cryptographic hashes verify integrity.

2. Legal says a lawsuit is likely. IT's 30-day mailbox purge should?

  • a) Continue
  • b) Be suspended for relevant data under a legal hold
  • c) Be accelerated
  • d) Be ignored by IR
answer

b) Legal hold overrides routine deletion.

short lab: Evidence checklist

A workstation shows signs of data theft. List what you collect in order, and how you document it.

walkthrough

RAM image first (volatile), then running processes/network connections, then a disk image via write blocker, plus relevant remote logs (proxy, DNS, EDR, authentication). Hash everything with SHA-256, record in chain-of-custody forms (who, when, where, why), store in locked evidence storage and work only on copies.
