study --objective 1.6 --exam CS0-004

CS0-004domain 1.0 · Security Operations1.6

1.6 Summarize concepts related to the use of AI in security operations

study notes

  • Use cases: compare artifacts (this script versus known samples), analyze log files at volume, draft documents and reports, assist incident investigation, correlate events across sources, and drive automation and orchestration.
  • Risks: hallucinations (confident wrong answers, so verify before acting), data exposure (pasting logs, secrets or customer data into a third-party model), model poisoning (tampered training or feedback data corrupts the model), malicious prompts (prompt injection hidden in emails, tickets or web pages that an AI agent reads).
  • Governance: an AI usage policy (what data may go in, which tools are approved), legal and regulatory compliance (privacy, sector rules), human review of AI output, logging of AI actions.
  • Rule of thumb: AI proposes, the analyst decides. Treat model output as an untrusted junior colleague's work.

flashcards

self-check

1. An AI summary of an incident cites a CVE that doesn't exist. Which risk?

  • a) Model poisoning
  • b) Hallucination
  • c) Data exposure
  • d) Prompt injection
answer

b) Fabricated detail presented as fact.

2. An email contains hidden text telling the SOC's AI triage bot to mark it as safe. This is?

  • a) Prompt injection
  • b) A hash collision
  • c) SQL injection
  • d) Beaconing
answer

a) Adversarial instructions embedded in input.

short lab: Make a safe-use rule

Write three rules for what an analyst may paste into an AI assistant.

walkthrough

Examples: never paste credentials, tokens or customer personal data; only sanitized or synthetic log excerpts; only use the company-approved tool; verify every claim against the source before it enters a report.
