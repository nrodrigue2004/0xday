study --objective 2.3 --exam CS0-004

CS0-004domain 2.0 · Vulnerability Management2.3

2.3 Given a scenario, analyze data to prioritize and mitigate vulnerabilities

study notes

You cannot fix everything. Prioritize on real risk.

Criteria

  • Exploitability (is there a public exploit, how easy), active exploitation (CISA KEV list, threat intel: highest priority), asset value and impact (crown jewels versus a test box), patch availability, true/false positives and negatives (TP real, FP false alarm, FN missed vuln, which is the scary one).
  • CVSS: base score 0-10 from Attack Vector (network is worst), Attack Complexity, Privileges Required, User Interaction, Scope, then C/I/A impact. Severity bands: None 0, Low 0.1-3.9, Medium 4-6.9, High 7-8.9, Critical 9-10. Temporal and environmental metrics adjust for the real world. EPSS is a probability (0-1) that a CVE will be exploited in the next 30 days. Use CVSS for severity and EPSS for likelihood.
  • Context: internet-facing (external) first, then internal, then isolated/air-gapped. A medium vuln on the perimeter can outrank a critical on an isolated lab box.

Mitigation

  • Attack surface management: continuously discover and shrink exposed assets and services.
  • Secure coding: input validation, parameterized queries, output encoding, least privilege, secrets out of code.
  • Patching and configuration management: test, stage, deploy, verify. Exceptions: documented, time-boxed, approved acceptance with an owner. Compensating controls: WAF rule, segmentation, virtual patching, extra monitoring when you cannot patch.
  • Validation: rescan, retest or verify evidence afterward. A closed ticket is not a fixed vulnerability.

flashcards

self-check

1. Two vulns: CVSS 9.8 on an isolated lab VM, CVSS 7.5 on an internet-facing web server actively exploited per threat intel. Which first?

  • a) The 9.8 lab VM
  • b) The 7.5 web server
  • c) Neither
  • d) Alphabetical
answer

b) Exposure plus active exploitation outweigh a higher raw score.

2. A vendor patch breaks a legacy app. Best interim?

  • a) Ignore it
  • b) Compensating controls with a documented, time-limited exception
  • c) Delete the app
  • d) Turn off logging
answer

b) Reduce risk and track acceptance formally.

short lab: Rank four findings

Rank: (A) critical RCE on internal print server, no exploit known. (B) medium SQLi on internet-facing portal with working Metasploit module. (C) high missing patch on isolated lab network. (D) low info leak on public site.

walkthrough

B first (exposed, exploit exists), then A (critical, internal, may be reached after a foothold), then C, then D. Say it as: exposure and exploitation beat raw severity.
