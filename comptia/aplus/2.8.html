2.8 Network Tools

study notes

Network Tools

  • Crimper: attaches a connector (RJ45 for twisted pair, coax or fiber types) by pushing prongs through insulation into the copper. Pair with a wire stripper and cable snips/electrician scissors. Use the right connector for the cable category; keep twists and the strain-relief tab in the sheath.
  • Wi-Fi analyzer: software or hardware that shows frequencies, channels and interference; aim antennas by watching the result.
  • Tone generator + inductive probe (toner probe): tone on one end, probe near cables to find it in a bundle.
  • Punch down tool: seats each wire in the slot of a punch down block and trims the excess. Keep the twist, label and number each connection, and document the matching number on the other end.
  • Cable tester: two units, continuity test (pin 1 to 1 ... missing, shorted or crossed). No crosstalk or loss data; use a TDR (time domain reflectometer) for lengths, breaks and signal loss.
  • Loopback plug: sends a signal out an interface and back in to test the port (serial, Ethernet, T1, fiber). Not a crossover cable.
  • Network tap: physical tap sits inline and copies traffic to an analyzer; passive (fiber) or active (copper, powered). Port mirror / SPAN on a switch copies traffic to another port; limited bandwidth, temporary use.

flashcards

self-check

1. Which tool tells you a cable is 12 m long with a break at 9 m?

  • a) Cable tester
  • b) TDR
  • c) Loopback plug
  • d) Crimper
answer

b) TDR measures distance to a fault.

2. You need to test whether a NIC port itself is faulty. Use:

  • a) Loopback plug
  • b) Punch down tool
  • c) Toner
  • d) Crossover cable
answer

a) It returns the port's own signal.

short lab: Terminate a cable

List the steps to make a Cat 6 patch cable and how to confirm it.

walkthrough

Strip the sheath, untwist minimal length, arrange in T568A or B order, trim even, insert to the stop with the right connector (Cat 6 rated), crimp, then test on a cable tester for pins 1 to 8 in order. Label it.
