2.7 Internet Connections

study notes

Internet Connection Types

  • Satellite (non-terrestrial, low earth orbit): expensive, e.g. 50 Mb/s down, 3 Mb/s up; older geostationary latency about 250 ms each way, Starlink about 40 ms and falling; works where nothing else does; line of sight and rain fade.
  • Fiber: very high speed and long distance, expensive equipment and repair; fiber to the home/business.
  • Cable broadband: DOCSIS, video, voice and data on the same coax by using different frequencies; 1 Gb/s or more.
  • DSL / ADSL: existing phone lines; asymmetric (about 200 Mb/s down, 20 Mb/s up in the example); limited to about 10,000 feet from the CO.
  • Cellular: phone network; tethering (USB or Bluetooth) or hotspot (802.11).
  • WISP: wireless internet service provider; ground stations, common in rural areas; external antenna; up to about 1,000 Mb/s.

Network Types

  • LAN (local, Ethernet and 802.11, high bandwidth). WAN (cities, countries; slower; point-to-point serial, MPLS, fiber, satellite). PAN (personal: Bluetooth, infrared, NFC; earbuds, car, fitness). MAN (one metro area, typically metro Ethernet; common for governments). SAN (storage area network, high-speed block-level access to central storage). WLAN (wireless LAN, 802.11 in a building).

flashcards

self-check

1. A farm with no cable or DSL, but many nearby ground-based radio towers. Option?

  • a) WISP
  • b) ADSL
  • c) DOCSIS
  • d) MAN
answer

a) Wireless ISP serves rural areas.

2. Wireless earbuds and a phone form a:

  • a) LAN
  • b) PAN
  • c) WAN
  • d) SAN
answer

b) Personal area network.

short lab: Match the connection

Pick a link for (1) 1 Gb symmetric to a business, (2) remote cabin with no cable, (3) laptop on the road with a phone.

walkthrough

(1) Fiber. (2) Satellite (or WISP). (3) Hotspot or tether over cellular.
