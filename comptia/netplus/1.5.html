1.5 Transmission Media

study notes

Wireless Networking

  • IEEE 802.11 is the Wi-Fi standard. The Wi-Fi Alliance certifies interoperability (the Wi-Fi logo). Generation names: 802.11n = Wi-Fi 4, 802.11ac = Wi-Fi 5, 802.11ax = Wi-Fi 6 (6E adds the 6 GHz band), 802.11be = Wi-Fi 7. Older a/b/g are rarely used. Bands: 2.4, 5 and 6 GHz. Theoretical link rates rise each generation (antenna and radio counts change the numbers).
  • Cellular: 4G LTE (long term evolution, merged GSM and CDMA worlds) about 150 Mbps, LTE-A about 300 Mbps. 5G (from 2020) target up to 10 Gbps, real-world 100 to 900 Mbps, which makes cellular a realistic wired substitute and enables big IoT deployments.
  • Satellite: for sites with no terrestrial option. Roughly 100 Mbps down and 5 Mbps up. Needs line of sight, so storms cause rain fade. Classic latency is about 250 ms each way, newer low-orbit service (Starlink) advertises about 40 ms and falling.

Ethernet Standards

  • Ethernet is defined by IEEE 802.3. Name decoding: 1000BASE-T = speed (1000 Mbps), BASE = baseband (one frequency, versus broadband), suffix = media. T twisted pair copper, F/SX fiber (SX is short-wavelength light). 10GBASE-T = 10 Gbps over copper. The name hints at the standard but you must read the standard for the exact details.

Optical Fiber

  • Light over fiber: hard to tap without notice, signal survives far longer than copper (km versus 100 m), immune to radio interference. Parts: light source (LED or laser), core (reflective), cladding (low reflectivity), buffer coating.
  • Multimode: wide core, several light modes, cheap LED light source, shorter runs (up to about 2 km). Single-mode: tiny core, one mode, laser, very long runs (up to about 100 km without regeneration).

Copper Cabling

  • Twisted pair: pairs carry opposite-polarity copies of the signal (TX+/TX-, RX+/RX-); twisting means interference hits both wires and is cancelled at the receiver. Each pair has a different twist rate. A cable has no "speed": the Ethernet standard and signal define the speed, and the category (Cat 5, 6, 7...) sets the minimum quality (1000BASE-T needs Cat 5 or better).
  • Coax: center conductor, insulator, shield, jacket, all on one axis. RG-6 is the cable-TV/cable-modem coax. Twinax: two conductors, used for short 10 Gb SFP+ links (about 5 m), cheap and low latency.
  • Plenum: the air space above a drop ceiling. If it is shared air (no ductwork walls), cables there must be plenum-rated (FEP or low-smoke PVC jacket) so a fire does not pump toxic smoke through the building. Stiffer than normal cable.

Network Transceivers

  • A transceiver (transmitter + receiver) is a swappable module so one switch port can be copper or fiber, at the speed you need. Must match the switch type (Ethernet versus Fibre Channel).
  • SFP: small form-factor pluggable, 1 Gbps. SFP+: same size, up to 10 Gbps (spec says to about 16). QSFP: four SFP channels in a slightly larger module, 4 Gbps. QSFP+: four SFP+ channels, 40 Gbps (can be split into four 10G links).

Fiber Connectors

  • SC (subscriber/square/standard): push-pull, snaps in. LC (local/Lucent/little): smaller, latch clip you press to release, very common now. ST (straight tip): round bayonet, push and twist. MPO (multi-fiber push on, branded MTP): 12 fibers in one push-pull connector, for density. Fibers usually come in pairs, one transmit, one receive.

Copper Connectors

  • RJ11: 6 positions, 2 conductors used (6P2C), analog phone and DSL. RJ45: 8 positions, 8 conductors, Ethernet. F-connector: threaded, coax for cable TV and cable modems (DOCSIS). BNC: bayonet-style coax connector (push, twist), named after Neill and Concelman.

flashcards

self-check

1. A run must go 40 km between two buildings. Which medium?

  • a) Cat 6
  • b) Multimode fiber
  • c) Single-mode fiber
  • d) Twinax
answer

c) Only single-mode fiber with a laser reaches that far.

2. A rack needs a 40 Gbps port from a single module. Which transceiver?

  • a) SFP
  • b) SFP+
  • c) QSFP+
  • d) RJ45
answer

c) Four SFP+ channels give 40 Gbps.

3. Cabling goes above a drop ceiling with open shared air. Which jacket?

  • a) PVC
  • b) Plenum-rated FEP or low-smoke PVC
  • c) Any shielded cable
  • d) Coax
answer

b) Required for fire safety in air-handling spaces.

short lab: Pick the cable

For each need choose media and connector: (a) office desk 1 Gbps, (b) rack-to-rack 10 Gbps, 3 m, (c) between buildings 12 km, (d) cable internet into a modem.

walkthrough

(a) Cat 5e/6 with RJ45, 1000BASE-T. (b) Twinax SFP+ DAC (or SFP+ fiber). (c) Single-mode fiber, LC pair. (d) Coax RG-6 with an F-connector.
