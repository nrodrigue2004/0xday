2.4 Physical Installations

study notes

Installing Networks

  • A distribution frame is where cables are passively terminated (punch-down blocks, patch panels). MDF (main distribution frame): the central room (usually the data center) where WAN circuits and internal LAN runs come together and the core gear lives. IDF (intermediate distribution frame): a satellite closet per floor or building that connects back to the MDF.
  • Racks: 19 inches wide, height in rack units (U) of 1.75 inches each, typically 42U tall, depth varies. Lockable cabinets add physical security and need ventilation.
  • Cooling: an HVAC designed for the heat load, with enough power, and tied into the fire system (shuts off if fire). Hot aisle / cold aisle: equipment faces so servers pull cold air from the cold aisle and exhaust to the hot aisle (cold air often through a raised floor, hot air drawn out of the ceiling). Cold aisles are often enclosed.
  • Cabling practice: run one cable from each desk to a patch panel (110 punch-down block on the back, RJ45 jacks on the front) and never touch it again. Moves, adds and changes are done with patch cables between the panel and the switch. Fiber panels: respect the bend radius and leave a service loop of slack for future changes.

Power

  • Safety: disconnect power before working, and remember capacitors in CRT monitors and laser printers can still shock you. Never ground yourself to an energized circuit.
  • Units: amps (current, the hose diameter), volts (pressure, 120 V or 240 V), watts = volts x amps (120 V x 0.5 A = 60 W). AC (wall power: 110-120 V at 60 Hz in the US, 220-240 V at 50 Hz in Europe) is converted to DC inside device power supplies.
  • UPS: batteries ride through outages and even out brownouts (low voltage) and surges. Offline/standby (cheapest, tiny gap that can reboot switches), line-interactive (boosts during brownouts), online/double-conversion (always on battery, no gap, preferred in data centers). Features: auto shutdown, capacity, outlets.
  • PDU: power distribution unit, rack-mounted, often intelligent: IP-managed with a built-in web server, per-outlet remote power on/off.

Environmental Factors

  • Data centers use about 2 percent of US electricity, much of it for the environment. Ideal humidity 40 to 60 percent (too high: condensation, too low: static discharge). Temperature roughly 64 to 81 degrees F. HVAC with sensors keeps it even.
  • Fire suppression: no water on live equipment. Use inert gas or chemical agents that smother the fire (clean agent systems), integrated with HVAC shutdown to cut off oxygen.

flashcards

self-check

1. A switch reboots during brief power blips even though it has a UPS. Most likely UPS type?

  • a) Online double-conversion
  • b) Offline/standby
  • c) Line-interactive with AVR
  • d) PDU
answer

b) The transfer gap is long enough to reboot sensitive gear.

2. You must reach a fiber patch panel that was bent tightly. What risk?

  • a) Increased humidity
  • b) Exceeded bend radius damaging the fiber
  • c) Over-voltage
  • d) DFS
answer

b) Fiber breaks or loses signal when bent past its radius.

short lab: Power math

A rack draws 12 A at 208 V. How many watts, and what UPS rating (in VA) would you at least need?

walkthrough

12 x 208 = 2,496 W. UPS VA is roughly watts divided by a power factor of about 0.9 plus headroom, so choose about 3 kVA or more (more if you want runtime or growth).
