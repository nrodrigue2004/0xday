5.4 Performance Issues

study notes

Performance Issues

  • Congestion: more traffic arrives than a link can send (two 1 Gb links into one 1 Gb uplink). Small buffers fill and packets are dropped. Fix: faster links, more links, or less traffic (QoS).
  • Bottleneck: the slowest component limits everything (bus, CPU, storage, slowest link). Drill down: the example web transaction at about 1,500 to 1,750 ms was dominated by the database; a database fix cut it to about 500 ms.
  • Bandwidth (utilization) percentage over time, throughput actual data moved; gather with the switch, SNMP or NetFlow.
  • Latency: delay between request and response; measure at each hop with packet captures (microsecond resolution).
  • Packet loss/discards: valid packets dropped by congestion or outage; corruption (bad cable, weak Wi-Fi) forces retransmission.
  • Jitter: variation in the time between packets. Voice and live video need regular spacing; high jitter causes clicks and stutters.

Wireless Issues

  • Interference and channels: 2.4 GHz has only three non-overlapping channels in North America (1, 6, 11). Channel 8 would overlap 6 and 11. 5 GHz and 6 GHz have many more. Let APs auto-select channels; recheck manual ones.
  • Best practices: disable legacy/low-speed support, lower output power on small areas, split big networks across APs on different channels, upgrade to newer standards.
  • Signal attenuation: weaker with distance. Use a Wi-Fi analyzer and heat map; raise power (if supported), add a higher-gain antenna, keep coax runs short and undamaged. Run site surveys repeatedly (neighbors change).
  • Client disassociation: older 802.11 management frames are unprotected, so an attacker can kick clients off (DoS). Look for deauth frames in a capture; newer standards (protected management frames) prevent it.
  • Roaming: multiple APs with the same SSID must have identical settings and security, or roaming clients get dropped.

flashcards

self-check

1. Voice calls stutter though bandwidth is plentiful and ping averages are fine. Which metric?

  • a) Jitter
  • b) Attenuation
  • c) MTU
  • d) TTL
answer

a) Uneven packet spacing hurts real-time audio.

2. Two APs share 2.4 GHz. Which channel pair avoids overlap?

  • a) 1 and 6
  • b) 6 and 8
  • c) 3 and 5
  • d) 9 and 10
answer

a) 1, 6 and 11 are non-overlapping.

3. Laptops repeatedly drop off Wi-Fi and a capture shows many deauth frames. Probable cause?

  • a) Client disassociation attack
  • b) DNS poisoning
  • c) CRC errors
  • d) Giants
answer

a) Forged management frames.

short lab: Fix the channel plan

Three APs in one building use 2.4 GHz channels 1, 4 and 9. Complaints are about slow Wi-Fi. What do you change?

walkthrough

Channels 4 and 9 overlap with their neighbors. Use 1, 6 and 11, or move clients to 5 GHz or 6 GHz, and lower power on APs that cover small areas.
