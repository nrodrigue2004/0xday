5.2 Physical Issues

study notes

Cable Issues

  • Fiber mismatch: single-mode (core about 9 microns, one light path) vs multimode (50 or 62.5 micron core, many paths), all with 125 micron cladding, so they look identical. Mixing them causes signal errors. Colors are not reliable; read the jacket print and document your fiber. Transceivers must also match the wavelength (850 nm multimode, 1310 nm single-mode).
  • Copper categories (set by TIA, minimums come from IEEE): 1000BASE-T needs Cat 5 up to 100 m; 10GBASE-T needs Cat 6 (UTP about 55 m) or Cat 6A (100 m). Bandwidth = theoretical maximum; throughput = what you actually move. Wrong category means errors and slowdowns.
  • UTP vs STP: STP has a shield (grounded) around the cable or each pair; use it near interference.
  • Crosstalk: leaking between pairs. NEXT near-end, FEXT far-end, alien from other cables. ACR compares attenuation to near-end crosstalk, like signal to noise (higher is better). Fix: re-terminate, keep twists up to the connector, use Cat 6A with spacers.
  • EMI: keep cables away from power lines, fluorescent lights, generators and fire systems; never break the shield. Respect bend radius, no staples, use Velcro ties, not tight plastic ones.
  • Attenuation: loss of signal over distance (copper, fiber, Wi-Fi); explains length limits.
  • Bad terminations: pins must match end to end (1-1, 2-2...). Wrong pins drop speed (1 Gb falls to 100 Mb), reversed pairs give no link, crossed pairs (half cross) appear as 1-3 and 3-1. Auto-MDIX may hide this, but terminate properly. Always test with a cable tester.

Interface Issues

  • Monitor with SNMP (standard counters in MIB-II, plus vendor MIBs). Watch link status (up/down), utilization and errors.
  • Ethernet frame: preamble/SFD, destination MAC, source MAC, EtherType, payload, FCS. A bad FCS = CRC error (cable, interface, duplex or interference). Runt = under 64 bytes (collisions on half duplex). Giant = over 1,518 bytes (or over the configured jumbo size). Drops = no buffer room (congestion).
  • Error disabled: the switch shut a port itself (flapping link, port security violation, errors). It stays down until an admin re-enables it. Administratively down: an admin turned it off. Suspended: incompatible configuration at once, for example LACP on one side only.

Hardware Issues

  • PoE: power on data wires. Source: endspan (PoE switch) or midspan (injector). Standards: PoE 802.3af 15.4 W, PoE+ 802.3at 25.5 W, PoE++ 802.3bt type 3 up to 51 W and type 4 up to 71.3 W (cameras, laptops). Check device class and the switch's total PoE budget (such as 200 W or 720 W).
  • Transceiver mismatches: wavelength and fiber type must agree; labels are small and hidden once inserted.
  • Power budget: transmit power (dBm) minus loss (distance, connectors, splices) = received power; it must be above the receiver sensitivity. Example: sensitivity -17 dBm; receiving -15 dBm is fine, -20 dBm is too weak.

flashcards

self-check

1. A switch port went down after a user unplugged a desktop and plugged in a personal laptop. Status?

  • a) Error disabled (port security)
  • b) Administratively down
  • c) Suspended
  • d) Full duplex
answer

a) The violation shut the port; re-enable it manually.

2. A 10GBASE-T run of 80 m on Cat 6 UTP drops packets. Most likely cause?

  • a) Cat 6 UTP only supports about 55 m at 10G
  • b) DHCP
  • c) A runt
  • d) NTP
answer

a) Use Cat 6A or shorten.

3. Interface counters show a growing CRC count. Where do you look first?

  • a) Cable and connectors
  • b) DNS
  • c) VLAN IDs
  • d) DHCP scope
answer

a) CRC errors point to the physical layer.

4. Receiver sensitivity is -17 dBm and the link delivers -20 dBm. Result?

  • a) Fine
  • b) Too weak, signal loss
  • c) Too strong
  • d) Needs PoE
answer

b) Received power is below sensitivity.

short lab: Read the counters

An interface shows 5 runts, 0 giants, 4821 CRC, 12 drops, link up, half duplex on one end and full on the other. Diagnose.

walkthrough

A duplex mismatch causes collisions (runts), CRC errors and drops. Set both ends to auto-negotiate or the same fixed speed and duplex, replace the cable if the CRC count continues to rise.
