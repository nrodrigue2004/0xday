4.5 Environmental Impacts

study notes

Disposal and Documentation

  • MSDS / SDS (Material Safety Data Sheet, US OSHA requirement) accompanies hazardous products (toner, ink, batteries): ingredients, hazards, first aid, firefighting, disposal. Consult it for disposal guidance.
  • Batteries (laptop, phone, UPS) and toner cartridges go to proper recycling or hazardous waste, never normal garbage. Many manufacturers include return labels; some stores give discounts.

Room Conditions

  • Control temperature (cooling for CPUs and RAM) and humidity (about 50% balance: higher reduces ESD, lower is comfortable and prevents condensation). Ventilation and dust: vacuum indoors; compressed air outdoors for very dusty equipment.

Power Protection

  • UPS provides backup power and protects against brownouts and surges; building UPS can fill a room. Types: standby (switches to battery after a small delay), line interactive (battery assists as voltage drops), online (always runs from battery/inverter, mains charges it). Features: auto shutdown, capacity (VA), outlet count, line suppression.
  • Surge suppressor: redirects excess voltage to ground and filters noise. Ratings: joules (200 good, 400 better, 600+ best), clamping UL 1449 let-through rating (500/400/330 V; lower is better), amp rating, noise filtering in dB.

flashcards

self-check

1. You need to learn how to dispose of a toner cartridge safely. Consult:

  • a) The MSDS/SDS
  • b) The SLA
  • c) The AUP
  • d) The CMDB
answer

a) Safety data sheet.

2. Needs zero transfer delay on power loss. Choose:

  • a) Online UPS
  • b) Standby UPS
  • c) Surge strip
  • d) Power strip
answer

a) No switchover gap.

3. Better surge suppressor spec:

  • a) 600 joules, 330 V let-through
  • b) 200 joules, 500 V let-through
  • c) 100 joules
  • d) No rating
answer

a) Higher J, lower V.
